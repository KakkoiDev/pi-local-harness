Designing a timetable you can read at a glance

Three things we changed after watching people use the trip planner on a crowded platform.

By Hana Ito 18 Oct 2026 6 min read Updated 19 Oct 2026

Big times, small words

People read the departure time first and the platform second. So the time is the largest thing on the row, and the platform number sits right beside it.1

  1. Departure time, large
  2. Platform, beside the time
  3. Line and stops, small

“I only look for two things: when, and which platform.”

— A commuter at Kyoto Station

Times in the data, not in the view

We store times with their time zone and format them only when we show them: Intl.DateTimeFormat.

const fmt = new Intl.DateTimeFormat("ja-JP", { hour: "2-digit", minute: "2-digit" });
fmt.format(new Date("2026-10-18T09:52:00+09:00")); // "09:52"

Notes

  1. Measured with 12 people in September 2026; see the study notes. ↩
